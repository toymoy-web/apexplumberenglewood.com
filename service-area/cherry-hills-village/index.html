Plumbing on large Cherry Hills lots

Cherry Hills Village was incorporated in 1945 and covers about 6.3 square miles, with roughly 6,400 residents at the 2020 census. It was named for the orchards it replaced, and commercial development has long been kept out, leaving estates on large lots, unpaved lanes and bridle paths along the High Line Canal.

Big lots change plumbing in practical ways. Water service lines and sewer laterals are often much longer than in Englewood, which makes leak detection and trenchless repair methods especially valuable. Mature trees are everywhere, and their roots find sewer joints the same way they do in older Englewood neighborhoods. Irrigation systems are nearly universal too, and the IPC (International Plumbing Code) requires backflow protection on lawn irrigation connections, which is why those devices need testing and winterizing.

Inside, larger homes tend to have more bathrooms, bigger or multiple water heaters, hot water recirculation pumps, and hydronic boiler systems with several zones.

Emergency calls from the Village go out around the clock, including to long private drives and guest houses set well back from the road.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

Four quick steps for any Cherry Hills Village property.

Shut off the main valve

Turn the main off where the service line enters the house, plus the separate valves for any guest house, pool equipment or irrigation.

Kill power to wet areas

In finished lower levels, switch off circuits feeding outlets and lights near the water at the breaker panel.

Clear the immediate area

Move rugs, artwork and electronics off wet floors, since restoration costs rise quickly with higher-end finishes.

What affects plumbing costs in Cherry Hills Village

Prices are set the same way as elsewhere, but large homes add a few factors.

Length of underground lines

Long service lines and laterals make excavation and replacement larger jobs.

Finish level

High-end finishes around a leak can cost more to open and restore than the pipe repair.

Number of systems

Multiple heaters, zones and fixtures mean more to diagnose and maintain.

Time of the call

Emergency calls after hours carry higher rates.

Many policies pay for sudden water damage but not the failed pipe or gradual leaks. High-value homes may have separate coverage for water damage, so check limits before a loss.
