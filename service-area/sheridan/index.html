A small city with river and creek plumbing concerns

Sheridan is a compact home rule city of about 2.3 square miles in Arapahoe County, incorporated back in 1890. The 2020 census counted roughly 6,100 residents. It is small enough that most of the city is only a few minutes from central Englewood.

Two waterways shape Sheridan. Bear Creek crosses the city on its way to the South Platte River, and the river itself runs along Sheridan's eastern side. The state and FEMA have mapped floodplains for both, which tells you water tables and flood risk are part of life near them. The IPC (International Plumbing Code) does not allow groundwater from a sump to be pumped into a sewer meant only for sewage, so sump discharge should go outside.

For plumbing, that means basements closer to the creek and river are the ones most likely to need a working sump pump, and the ones most affected when a sewer line backs up during heavy rain.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

Four quick steps for any Sheridan property.

Shut off the main valve

Most Sheridan houses have the main shutoff on the basement wall facing the street. Close it fully before anything else.

Kill power to wet areas

Basements near Bear Creek flood fastest, so switch off breakers for any circuit near standing water, if the panel is dry to reach.

Clear the immediate area

Lift boxes, rugs and electronics off the floor and keep children and pets away from the water.
