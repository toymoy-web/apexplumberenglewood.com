Old houses, new houses and the same old sewer

University Park is a Denver neighborhood of roughly 8,000 residents around the University of Denver, which began in 1864 as the Colorado Seminary. Tree-lined blocks near Observatory Park and McWilliams Park still hold many 1940s and 1950s bungalows and brick Tudors.

Many original homes have been replaced by large new builds in recent years. That creates an unusual mix on a single block: galvanized supply pipe and clay sewer laterals in one house, PEX and PVC next door, and new construction tying into old utility connections along the street.

The mature trees that make the neighborhood attractive also send roots into older sewer laterals, so repeated backups in the original houses are a familiar call.

Across University Park, from Observatory Park to the student rentals near campus, emergency dispatch runs around the clock.

Four quick steps for any University Park property.

Kill power to wet areas

Rentals and winter break

With a large share of rentals near campus, University Park sees a specific winter problem: houses left mostly empty over the winter break with the heat turned low. A cold snap during that time can freeze kitchen lines on exterior walls and outdoor faucets that were never disconnected.

Owners can prevent most of it by keeping the heat at a moderate setting, having someone check the house during cold spells, and disconnecting hoses before students leave. If a line does freeze, our frozen pipe repair service thaws and checks it safely.
