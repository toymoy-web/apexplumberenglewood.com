A 1950s neighborhood built almost overnight

Harvey Park was annexed into Denver in 1954, and most of its homes went up between 1954 and 1965. Thousands of houses were built in just a few years to meet post-war demand, which means whole streets share the same age of pipe, the same drain layouts and the same original fixtures.

The neighborhood is best known for its 170 Cliff May homes, prefabricated mid-century houses built with the Cliff May and Chris Choate system and shown in the 1955 Parade of Homes. Their post-and-beam construction and open floor plans make running new pipe a careful job, since interior walls and ceilings are part of the design.

At the center of the neighborhood are Harvey Lake and the Harvey Park Recreation Center, with Riviera and Wolcott lakes nearby. To the south, Harvey Park South is changing as the 70-acre Loretto Heights campus is redeveloped.

Across Harvey Park, from the Cliff May blocks to the homes around Harvey Lake, emergency plumbers go out around the clock.

Denver water, Denver permits

Harvey Park is inside the City and County of Denver, so water comes from Denver Water and permits come from Denver rather than Englewood. Denver's building code is based on the International Code Council's model codes, including the IPC (International Plumbing Code).

As in Englewood, the sewer lateral from the house to the city main is generally the owner's responsibility, so a backup that keeps returning is usually a private repair. Our sewer line repair page explains the options, and repiping covers the original galvanized supply lines still found in some homes.

For the post-and-beam Cliff May houses, leak detection matters more than usual, because opening a wall or ceiling in the wrong place is harder to put back than in a standard ranch.

Before We Arrive: If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

Four quick steps for any Harvey Park property.

Shut off the main valve

In most Harvey Park ranches the main shutoff is in the basement or utility room near where the line enters from the street. Close it fully.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is reaching outlets or a water heater, switch off those circuits at the panel, if you can reach it from a dry spot.

Clear the immediate area

Move rugs, furniture and stored items away from the water, and keep children and pets out of the room.
