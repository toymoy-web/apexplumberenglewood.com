Englewood, our home base

Englewood is a 6.6-square-mile city in Arapahoe County, directly south of Denver, with about 33,700 residents at the 2020 census. Its main ZIP codes are 80110 and 80113. Much of its housing dates from the years after World War II, so galvanized supply pipe, cast iron drains and clay sewer laterals are common.

Our Englewood emergency plumber page covers local issues in depth, including the city's lead service line program and who owns which part of the water and sewer lines.

Neighboring towns

Sheridan sits just west of Englewood and shares the 80110 ZIP code. Bear Creek and the South Platte River run through it, and River Point is its main retail center.

Cherry Hills Village borders Englewood to the east. It is almost entirely residential, with large lots, long service lines and larger homes with boilers, irrigation and pools.

Greenwood Village lies farther east and combines quiet neighborhoods with a large share of the Denver Tech Center, so it produces both residential and commercial calls.

University Park is a Denver neighborhood just north of Englewood, around the University of Denver. It is served by Denver Water and Denver permits rather than Englewood's, and its older bungalows share Englewood's galvanized pipe and sewer lateral issues.

Centennial is a large city incorporated in 2001. Many of its homes still have Englewood or Littleton mailing addresses. We focus on its western and central neighborhoods closest to Englewood.

Why water and sewer providers matter

Englewood runs its own water utility, Englewood Utilities, which treats water at the Allen Water Treatment Plant. The neighboring towns are different. Sheridan, Cherry Hills Village, Greenwood Village and Centennial are served by a patchwork of water and sanitation districts, and a single city can have several.

That matters for two practical reasons. First, the provider decides where its responsibility ends and yours begins on the water service line and the sewer lateral, which changes who pays for an underground repair. Second, different water sources have different mineral content, which affects scale in water heaters and whether a softener makes sense. The plumbing code itself does not change at the city line, since every city here works from the Colorado Plumbing Code, based on the IPC (International Plumbing Code).

When you call from outside Englewood, we check which provider serves your address before quoting underground work or recommending water treatment.

What to do when you call from any of these towns

These details help us dispatch the right plumber quickly.

  • Give your street address, not only the city, since several ZIP codes cross city lines.
  • Say whether water is still running and whether you have closed the main shutoff.
  • Mention if the property is a rental, business or HOA-managed building.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call Xcel Energy or 911 from outside first.
