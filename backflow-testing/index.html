Backflow Testing & Prevention in Englewood, CO

Backflow testing checks that a backflow prevention assembly still stops water from flowing backward into the public water supply. Backflow prevention covers installing and repairing those assemblies where a cross-connection exists. In Englewood, CO commercial and multifamily properties must submit an annual test each year by August 15 under the city's cross-connection program.

Got a notice from the city? Here is what it means

Most people look up backflow testing because a letter or email arrived. These are the usual reasons.

  • Your business or apartment building has a backflow assembly that needs its annual test.
  • An inspection found an uncontrolled cross-connection that needs an assembly installed.
  • A previous test failed and the assembly must be repaired and retested.
  • An irrigation system, fire sprinkler system or boiler feed has been added or changed.
  • You bought a commercial or multifamily property and inherited its assemblies.

If you need more time to complete a test, the city accepts extension requests. Asking early is better than missing the deadline.

Englewood's cross-connection program

The City of Englewood runs a Backflow Prevention and Cross-Connection Control program to keep contaminants from being drawn back into the drinking water system. Englewood Utilities requires commercial and multifamily properties to test their backflow assemblies every year and submit certification by August 15.

The city also inspects properties when needed to identify hazards. If an inspection finds an uncontrolled cross-connection, the property owner has 45 days to resolve it, unless an extension is approved.

The city lists irrigation systems, fire sprinkler systems, heating and cooling systems, industrial and medical systems, and swimming pools as common examples of cross-connections. Statewide, the Colorado Department of Public Health and Environment requires testable assemblies to be tested annually by a certified tester. The IPC (International Plumbing Code) sets the installation side, requiring backflow protection on connections such as lawn irrigation, boiler feeds and fire sprinkler systems.

What to do if you suspect a backflow event

Backflow is rare, but it can happen during a water main break or when a hose is left in a chemical bucket. These are the safe steps.

  • Stop drinking and cooking with tap water if it suddenly looks, smells or tastes unusual.
  • Remove any hose that is submerged in a pool, bucket, sprayer tank or tub.
  • Report the change to Englewood Utilities so the city can check the system.
  • Do not flush a commercial system or bypass an assembly until the cause is known.
  • Have the backflow assembly tested before normal use resumes.

What happens during a backflow test

  1. Schedule. A short water shutoff is arranged with the property, often outside business hours.
  2. Test the assembly. A certified tester uses a differential pressure gauge to check each check valve and relief valve.
  3. Repair if it fails. Worn rubber parts, springs or relief valves are rebuilt or replaced, then the assembly is retested.
  4. Submit results. The test report is submitted as the city requires, and you keep a copy for your records.

For properties with several assemblies, we can test them all in one visit. If the backflow issue is part of a broader building plumbing problem, the rest is handled through our commercial plumbing work.

What affects the cost of backflow work

Testing is a predictable cost. Repairs and installations vary more.

Number of assemblies

Each assembly is tested and reported separately.

Assembly size and type

Larger RPZ assemblies take longer to test and cost more to rebuild.

Repair parts

Rubber kits are inexpensive. Replacing a whole assembly costs more.

New installation

Adding an assembly to resolve a cross-connection may involve piping changes and a permit.

Scheduling

Tests outside business hours can reduce disruption for tenants or customers.

Backflow testing is a regulatory requirement and is not covered by insurance. Keeping test records current also helps if contamination is ever alleged.
