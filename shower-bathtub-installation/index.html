Shower valves and scald protection

Older shower valves often lack protection against sudden temperature changes. When someone flushes a toilet or runs a dishwasher, the cold water pressure drops and the shower can suddenly get very hot.

The IPC (International Plumbing Code) requires shower and tub-shower valves to be pressure-balancing, thermostatic or a combination, with a maximum outlet temperature limit. Replacing an old valve with a code-compliant one is one of the most useful safety upgrades in an older bathroom.

Since 2016, new showerheads sold in Colorado must carry the EPA WaterSense label, so a new showerhead will use less water than many older ones.

What to do before you call about a shower or tub

A few checks help us plan the job and quote it accurately.

  • If the shower leaks into a ceiling below, stop using it until it is checked.
  • Take photos of the tub, the valve trim and any access panel behind the shower.
  • Check if the basement ceiling below the bathroom is open or finished.
  • Note the tub size if you are replacing it, usually printed on the underside or measured along the front.
  • If the shower valve will not shut off, close the main water shutoff and call.

Leaks that show up below a bathroom are often from the drain or valve rather than the tub itself. Our leak detection page explains how we locate them.

Englewood bathrooms: what we often find

Many Englewood houses built after World War II have a single original bathroom with a cast iron or steel tub. Cast iron tubs are durable but heavy, and removing one takes planning and extra hands.

In basement homes, the tub drain is often reachable from below, which makes drain and trap replacements easier. In homes with finished basements, the ceiling may need to be opened, and we tell you before starting.

If the bathroom has old galvanized supply lines, replacing them while the wall is open is efficient. That work is covered on our repiping page.

Planning a tub-to-shower conversion

A walk-in shower that replaces a tub needs more than a new pan. The drain usually moves to suit the new base, the valve may be raised to shower height, and the walls behind need proper waterproofing before any tile goes up.

It is also the right moment to add solid blocking in the walls for grab bars, even if you do not need them yet. A low-threshold or curbless entry is easier to use at any age but takes more floor work to get the slope right, so we look at the joists or slab first.
