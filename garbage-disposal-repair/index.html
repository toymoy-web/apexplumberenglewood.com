What to do before you call about a disposal

Several disposal problems have safe fixes. Always switch the disposal off at the wall first.

  • Never put your hand into the disposal. Use tongs to remove visible objects.
  • If it hums but will not turn, it is jammed. Many units have a hex socket under the center of the unit for freeing the flywheel with the wrench that came with it.
  • Silent unit? Press the red reset button underneath it and check the breaker.
  • If it is leaking, turn it off, put a bucket under it, and do not keep running water.
  • Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a disposal. It can damage the unit and burn whoever repairs it.

Grease, the disposal and Englewood's sewers

A disposal grinds food into small particles, but it does nothing to grease. South Englewood Sanitation District No. 1 asks residents to wipe fats, oils and grease into the trash and warns that bleach poured down a drain can harden grease. Grease that goes down a disposal cools and coats the kitchen drain line.

That is why many kitchen drain clogs blamed on the disposal are really grease in the line behind it. A kitchen line that keeps clogging needs cleaning, and a heavily coated line is a good candidate for hydro jetting.

The IPC (International Plumbing Code) allows a dishwasher to drain through the disposal, but the hose has to be set up so dirty water cannot siphon back into the dishwasher, usually with a high loop or an air gap fitting. Fibrous foods such as celery and corn husks, along with pasta, rice and coffee grounds, are also hard on disposals and drains. Running plenty of cold water during and after use helps carry particles through.

Repair or replace, and choosing a new unit

Jams, reset trips and loose flanges are simple repairs. A disposal leaking from its body has an internal failure and needs to be replaced. Most disposals last roughly 8 to 15 years, depending on use and build quality.

Replacement units differ mainly in motor size and grind chamber material. A larger motor handles tougher scraps and jams less often. Stainless steel grinding parts resist corrosion better. Most replacements take an hour or two and reuse the existing electrical connection if it is in good condition.

What affects the cost of disposal work

Disposal jobs are short, but these factors change the price.

Repair or replace

Freeing a jam or resealing a flange costs less than a new unit.

Unit size and quality

Larger motors and stainless grinding parts cost more but last longer.

Electrical condition

A switch or cord that needs work adds time, and some fixes need an electrician.

Drain condition

A clogged drain line behind the disposal is cleared separately.

Timing

A disposal problem can usually wait for a weekday visit.

Disposal repair and replacement are maintenance and are not covered by homeowners insurance. Water damage from a sudden leak may be.
