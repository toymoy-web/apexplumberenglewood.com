Hydro Jetting in Englewood, CO

Hydro jetting cleans drain and sewer lines with a hose that sprays high-pressure water forward and backward from a specialized nozzle. It scours grease, scale, sludge and fine roots off the full inside wall of the pipe. In Englewood, CO it is the step after snaking when a line keeps clogging, and it is always paired with a camera look first.

Ask about hydro jetting

Snaked it and it clogged again: when jetting is the answer

People rarely search for hydro jetting by name. They search because a drain keeps coming back. These are the patterns that point to jetting.

  • A kitchen drain that clogs again within weeks of being snaked.
  • A main line that was cabled and the cable got stuck or came back wrapped in fine roots.
  • Grease or sludge visible on a camera, coating the pipe walls rather than blocking one spot.
  • Slow drains throughout a cast iron system with heavy internal scale.
  • A restaurant or commercial kitchen line that needs scheduled maintenance.

Jetting cleans pipe. It does not repair it. If the camera shows a cracked or collapsed section, that needs sewer line repair, not more cleaning.

Why cabling alone falls short

A cable machine spins a cutting head through the clog and opens a path for water. That is often all a one-time clog needs. But a cable is narrower than the pipe, so it leaves most of the grease or roots stuck to the walls, and the line narrows again as new material catches on what is left.

A jetting nozzle has rear-facing jets that pull the hose forward while scouring the pipe wall behind it, and a forward jet that breaks up the blockage ahead. Nozzles are chosen for the job: a penetrating nozzle for packed debris, a spinning nozzle for grease and scale, and a root-cutting head for fine root masses.

Pressure is matched to the pipe. A residential lateral in good condition tolerates far more than a thin, corroded cast iron branch, so pressure and nozzle choice come after the camera shows what the pipe can handle.

Grease in Englewood's sewers starts in the kitchen

South Englewood Sanitation District No. 1 asks residents to wipe fats, oils and grease into the trash. It also warns that pouring bleach into a drain can harden grease. South Platte Renew, the regional wastewater plant, runs an annual grease takeback program for the same reason.

Grease that coats a kitchen line inside the house often ends up in the lateral too. A jetted line stays clear far longer when the household stops sending grease down it. A worn garbage disposal adds to the problem, which our garbage disposal repair page covers.

How a jetting visit works

  1. Camera first. We inspect the line to confirm the pipe is intact and to see what is coating it.
  2. Pick nozzle and pressure. The nozzle and pressure are chosen for the pipe material, size and buildup.
  3. Jet from the cleanout. The hose is run from the cleanout, working in passes until water flows freely. The IPC (International Plumbing Code) requires cleanouts to stay accessible for exactly this reason.
  4. Camera again. A second camera run shows the cleaned pipe wall and any defects the buildup was hiding.
  5. Maintenance plan. For grease or root-prone lines, we suggest a realistic interval for the next cleaning.

What affects the price of hydro jetting

Jetting costs more than a single cable visit, but it often lasts much longer. These factors set the quote.

Line length and size

A long main line or a large commercial line takes more time and water than a short kitchen branch.

Cleanout access

A reachable cleanout saves time. Without one, adding a cleanout may be worth it.

Severity of buildup

Heavy grease or dense roots may need several passes and nozzle changes.

Camera inspection

Included before and after, because jetting a broken pipe does more harm than good.

Commercial scheduling

Restaurants often need after-hours work to avoid closing the kitchen.

Hydro jetting is maintenance and is not covered by homeowners insurance. For businesses, regular jetting records can help show a grease-related backup was not due to neglect.
