City requirements Englewood businesses deal with

The City of Englewood's cross-connection program requires commercial and multifamily properties to submit annual backflow assembly certification by August 15. If a city inspection finds an uncontrolled cross-connection, the owner has 45 days to resolve it unless an extension is granted. Our backflow testing page covers the test itself.

Food service kitchens produce fats, oils and grease, and the IPC (International Plumbing Code) calls for grease interceptors where that waste is produced. South Englewood Sanitation District No. 1 asks all customers to keep grease out of the sewer, and a neglected interceptor is a common cause of kitchen backups.

Permitted work, such as a new water heater, gas line or fixture layout change, goes through the City of Englewood's building permit process, with inspections scheduled around the business.

What to do if a commercial line backs up

A backup in a business affects customers, staff and sometimes neighbors. These steps limit the damage.

  • Stop using the affected fixtures and post a sign on restroom doors.
  • In a kitchen, stop running dish machines and prep sinks connected to the line.
  • Keep staff and customers away from sewage, and use gloves for any cleanup.
  • Find the building's main shutoff and the cleanout location if you can do so safely.
  • In a multi-tenant building, notify the property manager so other units stop using water.

Keeping downtime to a minimum

Businesses pay for plumbing problems twice, once in the repair and again in lost hours. We schedule planned work, such as jetting a kitchen line or replacing a water heater, before opening or after closing wherever possible.

Regular maintenance pays off in commercial buildings. Kitchen lines on a jetting schedule, water heaters flushed annually, and backflow assemblies tested on time prevent most emergency calls. Our hydro jetting service is the backbone of most restaurant maintenance plans.

Shutoff valves deserve attention too. In many older Englewood commercial buildings, the valves that isolate a restroom group or a tenant space have not been turned in years. When one finally fails to close, a simple fixture repair turns into a building-wide shutdown, so testing and replacing weak valves is part of any thorough service visit.

For multifamily buildings, one leak can affect several units. Leak detection narrows the source quickly, so walls are opened in one unit instead of three.
