Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Englewood, CO

Sump pump installation and repair covers the pump in a basement pit that removes groundwater before it can flood the floor. That includes replacing failed pumps, adding battery backups and fixing discharge lines. In Englewood, CO many homes have full basements, and a sump pump that fails during a heavy rain or spring snowmelt can flood one in hours.

Sump pump warning signs

  • Pump hums but does not pump.
  • Pump runs constantly.
  • Water near the pit or on the floor.
  • Discharge line frozen or blocked.

What to do if your sump pump stops working

A failed sump pump during a storm is urgent. These steps are safe to try first.

  • Check that the pump is plugged in and the outlet or breaker has power.
  • Do not stand in water to reach the pump or outlet. Switch off the circuit at the panel first.
  • Look in the pit for debris jamming the float switch and free it carefully with a stick.
  • Check the outside end of the discharge line for ice, snow or blockage.
  • If water is rising and the pump will not run, move valuables off the basement floor and call.

Why Englewood basements need working sump pumps

Englewood sits in the South Platte River valley, and groundwater near the river can be relatively shallow. Heavy spring rain, snowmelt and summer thunderstorms raise groundwater levels, and a sump pump keeps that water out of the basement.

The area knows flooding. The June 16, 1965 South Platte flood caused about $508 million in damage across the basin, most of it in the Denver metro, and Englewood was among the communities hit. Chatfield Dam was built afterward and now controls river floods, but it does nothing about groundwater under a basement floor.

Winter adds a local twist. Discharge lines that run across the yard can freeze during a hard cold snap, and a frozen line makes the pump run without moving any water.

Backups, discharge and the plumbing code

A battery backup pump takes over when power fails during a storm, which is exactly when a primary pump is needed most. An alarm that sounds when water rises in the pit gives warning before the floor gets wet.

Where the water goes matters too. The IPC (International Plumbing Code) does not allow storm water and groundwater to be drained into sewers meant only for sewage, so sump pumps should discharge outside, away from the foundation, rather than into a floor drain or laundry sink. If your pump is tied into the sanitary sewer, we can reroute it.

Water coming up through a floor drain is a different problem from groundwater, and it points to the sewer line. Our drain cleaning and sewer line repair pages cover that.

What affects the cost of sump pump work

Sump pump jobs are usually quick. These factors move the price.

Pump type

Submersible pumps cost more than pedestal pumps but are quieter and more common.

Backup system

Battery backups and water-powered backups add cost and protection.

Discharge line

Rerouting or burying a discharge line to prevent freezing adds work.

New pit

Installing a sump where there is none involves cutting the basement floor.

Emergency timing

Pumps fail during storms, and after-hours rates apply.

Many homeowners policies exclude water that backs up through a sump pump unless a sump or sewer backup rider was added. Check your policy before storm season.
