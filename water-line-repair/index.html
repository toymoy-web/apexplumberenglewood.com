Water Line Repair & Replacement in Englewood, CO

Water line repair and replacement covers the buried pipe that brings water from the street to your house. In Englewood, CO the city owns the line from the main to the curb stop, and the property owner owns the rest, from the curb stop into the home. This page is about that owner-side line, which is separate from the pipes inside your walls.

Who owns which part

  • Main to curb stop: City of Englewood.
  • Curb stop to house: property owner.
  • Confirmed lead lines: city replacement program.
  • Interior pipes: owner, covered by repiping.
Call about a water line

Soggy grass, weak pressure and rusty water from the street

A failing service line is buried, so the clues show up in the yard or at the tap.

  • A strip of lawn between the street and the house that stays wet or unusually green.
  • Water pooling near the curb, the meter pit or where the line enters the foundation.
  • Pressure that has dropped throughout the whole house rather than at one fixture.
  • Rusty water at every tap, including the first cold faucet after the main.
  • A meter that keeps moving with the main shutoff inside the house closed.

If water is bubbling up in the street or on the city side of the curb stop, report it to Englewood Utilities, because that part of the line is the city's to repair.

The lead program, galvanized lines and what is left to owners

Englewood's Lead Reduction Program replaces confirmed lead service lines with copper, from the water main all the way to just inside the house, and the city covers that work. Replacements began in 2024 and focus on homes built before 1960.

The program has a limit worth knowing. The city states that homes built after 1960 with galvanized steel service lines are not included, because those lines are not connected downstream of lead pipe. A 1960s or 1970s galvanized service line is still aging, though, and replacing it remains the owner's decision and cost.

Before calling anyone, you can look up your address on the city's online service line inventory map. It shows the recorded material of your line and whether it falls within the program.

Repair a section or replace the whole line

Service lines can be repaired in place or replaced end to end. The choice depends on the pipe material and how many problems it has had.

SituationUsual recommendationWhy
Copper line with one leak from a fitting or damageSpot repair.The rest of the line likely has years left.
Galvanized line with rust and low pressureFull replacement.Corrosion is throughout the pipe, not in one place.
Second leak on the same lineFull replacement.More leaks usually follow on an aging line.
Line under a driveway or mature treeTrenchless replacement where possible.It avoids breaking concrete or cutting major roots.

Trenchless methods pull or bore a new line along the old path with small pits at each end instead of a trench across the yard. Either way, the IPC (International Plumbing Code) sets rules for the new line, including keeping the water service separated from the building sewer, so the route is planned before digging. New lines are typically copper or approved plastic sized for the home's demand.

What a service line replacement involves

  1. Locate and confirm. We confirm the leak with a meter test and trace the line route from the curb stop to the foundation.
  2. Utility locates. Colorado 811 is contacted so gas, electric and communication lines are marked before digging.
  3. Permit. The work is permitted through the city, and the connection at the curb stop is coordinated as required.
  4. Install. The new line goes in by trench or trenchless method, with a new shutoff where it enters the house.
  5. Test and restore. The line is pressure tested and inspected, then the ground is backfilled and surfaces restored.

What sets the price of water line work

Service line jobs are quoted after the line is located, because what sits on top of the pipe matters as much as the pipe.

Length of the run

A short front yard costs far less than a long setback or a line that runs under a driveway.

Trench or trenchless

Trenchless methods can cost more per foot but avoid concrete and landscaping repair.

Surfaces to restore

Lawn is simple. Concrete walks, driveways and mature plantings add restoration cost.

Entry point

Where the line enters through a basement wall, the penetration must be sealed properly.

Emergency timing

A line leaking badly enough to require shutting off the house is handled 24/7.

Many homeowners policies exclude the underground service line itself. Some insurers and utilities offer separate service line coverage, so check what you have before you need it.
