Water Filtration Installation in Englewood, CO

Water filtration installation adds a certified filter to your plumbing to reduce specific contaminants or improve taste and odor. Options range from a filter under the kitchen sink to a whole-home system. In Englewood, CO the most common reasons are the city's lead service line work and the taste and odor of city water, and each calls for a different filter.

Match the filter to the goal

  • Lead: certified to NSF/ANSI 53.
  • Chlorine taste and odor: NSF/ANSI 42.
  • Reverse osmosis: NSF/ANSI 58.
  • Whole-home or single tap.
Ask about water filtration

Worried about lead, or just the taste?

The reason you want a filter decides which filter you need. These are the common starting points.

  • Your home was built before 1960 and may have a lead service line.
  • The city recently replaced your service line and you want ongoing filtration.
  • Your home has galvanized pipe inside the walls.
  • Your tap water tastes or smells of chlorine or earthiness.
  • You buy bottled water and would rather drink from the tap.

For lead, look for a filter certified to NSF/ANSI Standard 53 for lead reduction. For taste and odor, NSF/ANSI Standard 42 is the relevant certification.

Englewood's lead program and filters

The City of Englewood's Lead Reduction Program is replacing lead service lines with copper. Local reporting put the number of Englewood service lines expected to be lead or galvanized at about 3,000 out of 11,000.

After a lead service line replacement, Englewood Utilities provides a free water pitcher and filter to use for six months. The city recommends certified filters for drinking water, preparing infant formula, and cooking foods that absorb water, such as rice, beans and soup.

A pitcher is a good start, but a filter installed at the kitchen sink is more convenient for daily use. Homes that still have galvanized pipe inside the walls may want longer-term filtration, alongside considering repiping.

Just had your lead line replaced?

A certified drinking-water filter is a sensible step while interior plumbing is evaluated.

Taste and odor from city water

Englewood's water comes from City Ditch and the South Platte River and is treated at the Allen Water Treatment Plant. The city has a program aimed at improving taste, odor and hardness. Seasonal changes in source water can affect taste, and chlorine used for disinfection is noticeable to some people.

A carbon filter certified to NSF/ANSI 42 reduces chlorine taste and odor. For hardness, a filter is not the right tool, and our water softener page covers that.

How an under-sink filter goes in

  1. Pick the certification. We confirm the filter is certified for what you want to reduce, whether that is lead, chlorine taste or a broader list.
  2. Tap the cold line. The filter is connected to the cold supply under the kitchen sink, with its own shutoff so cartridges are easy to change.
  3. Add a faucet if needed. Some systems use a separate drinking-water faucet mounted on the sink or countertop.
  4. Flush and test. New cartridges are flushed as the manufacturer directs, and every connection is checked for leaks.
  5. Set a reminder. We note the cartridge model and replacement interval so the filter keeps performing as certified.

Reverse osmosis systems also need a drain connection and a small storage tank, so they take more cabinet space than a simple carbon filter.

What affects the cost of water filtration

Filtration systems vary widely. These factors move the price.

Filter type

An under-sink carbon filter costs less than a reverse osmosis or whole-home system.

Certification

Filters certified for lead cost more than basic taste filters.

Installation location

Under-sink installs are quick. Whole-home systems go near the main shutoff.

Filter replacement

Replacement cartridges are an ongoing cost, and schedules vary by product.

Plumbing changes

A dedicated faucet or drain connection for reverse osmosis adds work.

Water filtration is not covered by homeowners insurance.
