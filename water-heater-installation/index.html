Water Heater Installation & Replacement in Englewood, CO

Installing a water heater means removing of an old unit and the fitting of a new one. That can be a like-for-like tank swap, a move to tankless, or a switch to a heat pump model. In Englewood, CO a proper install also covers the permit, gas and venting checks at 5,348 feet, and the code items an inspector will look for. Signs it is time to replace rather than repair

Repair is often the right call, and our water heater repair page covers those cases. These are the signs the heater itself has reached the end.

  • Water leaking from the tank body or pooling under the heater with no loose fitting to blame.
  • A tank that is 10 to 12 years old or more, judged by the serial number on the label.
  • Rusty water from the hot side only, while cold water stays clear.
  • Loud rumbling or popping from heavy sediment that flushing no longer clears.
  • Repeated repairs within a year or two on an older unit.

Choosing the replacement: tank, tankless or heat pump

There is no single right answer. It depends on how much hot water the household uses, what the budget allows today, and whether gas or electric is already in place.

TypeStrengthsTrade-offs
Gas tank, 40 or 50 gallonLowest installed cost, fast recovery, familiar to service.Standby heat loss, 10 to 12 year typical life.

Englewood basements are often a good fit for a heat pump model because they have room and the unit's cool exhaust air is less noticeable there. If you are considering one, check Xcel Energy's current rebate offers before you decide. If you go tankless, plan on yearly tankless water heater service to keep scale down.

What a code-compliant install includes in Englewood

Swapping out a water heater counts as permitted work under the Colorado Plumbing Code, which is built on the 2021 IPC (International Plumbing Code). In Englewood the permit is issued through the City's Building Division, and an inspector checks the finished installation.

The items that most often fail inspection are small ones. A relief valve for temperature and pressure has to have a full-size discharge pipe running to within a few inches of the floor. Homes with a pressure reducing valve or a check valve on the main usually need a thermal expansion tank. Gas units need a sediment trap on the gas line and proper clearance to combustibles. A heater above a finished space needs a drain pan.

The orphaned water heater problem

In many older Englewood houses, the furnace and gas water heater once shared a masonry chimney. When the furnace is upgraded to a high-efficiency model that vents out the side wall, the water heater is left alone in a flue sized for two appliances. That can cause poor draft and condensation in the chimney. We check for this on every gas install and quote a liner or a different venting option if it applies.

How a replacement day usually goes

  1. Confirm the fit. We check the space, stairs and doorways, the gas line or electrical circuit, and the vent before the new unit is brought in.
  2. Drain and remove. Water, gas and power are shut off, the old tank is drained and carried out.
  3. Set and connect. The new heater is set, connected to water, gas or power, and vented, with any required code items added.
  4. Fill, light and test. The tank is filled before it is fired, then checked for leaks, draft and temperature.
  5. Walk-through. You see the shutoffs and the relief valve, and the permit inspection is scheduled.

Swapping one tank for a similar tank is normally a same-day job. Tankless conversions and relocations take longer because they may need new gas piping, venting or electrical work.

What affects the price of a water heater install

Every install is quoted before work begins. These are the factors that move the number.

Type and size

A 40-gallon gas tank costs far less than a large tankless unit or a heat pump model.

Code updates

Expansion tanks, relief valve piping, drain pans and sediment traps may be needed to meet current code.

Venting

An orphaned chimney may need a liner. Tankless and some tank models need new direct-vent piping.

Gas or electrical capacity

Tankless units often need a larger gas line. Heat pump models may need a dedicated circuit.

Access

A tight basement stairway or a closet install adds handling time.

Insurance does not pay for a new water heater because of age or wear. If a tank failure caused sudden water damage, that damage may be covered, so photograph it before cleanup.
