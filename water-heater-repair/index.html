Water Heater Repair in Englewood, CO

Water heater repair means getting an existing tank heater working again. Usually that means swapping the failed part, whether a thermocouple, gas control, element, thermostat or relief valve, or flushing out sediment. What to do first when there is no hot water

A few checks show if this needs a plumber tonight or can wait until morning.

  • Gas heater: look through the small window near the bottom of the tank. If there is no flame, the pilot or igniter has failed.
  • Electric heater: check the breaker for the water heater. If it has tripped more than once, leave it off and call.
  • Electric heater: some models have a reset button behind the upper panel. If it trips again soon after, there is a real fault.
  • Any heater: if water is pooling underneath, close the cold supply valve above the tank and turn off the gas or power.
  • Any heater: if you smell gas, leave the house and call Xcel Energy or 911 from outside before calling a plumber.

What usually fails on a tank water heater

Most repairs come down to a handful of parts. This is what we check first for each symptom.

SymptomLikely causeRepair or replace
Gas pilot will not stay litThermocouple or thermopileRepair. A quick, inexpensive part.
Gas burner will not fire at allGas control valve or flammable vapor sensorRepair on a younger tank, weigh replacement on an older one.
Electric heater, lukewarm waterFailed heating element or thermostatRepair. Both parts are replaceable.

Rotten egg smell from hot waterAnode rod reacting with the waterRepair. Replace the anode with a different type.
Leaking from the tank bodyCorroded tankReplace. Tanks cannot be patched.

The relief valve is the one part never to bypass or plug. The IPC (International Plumbing Code), which the Colorado Plumbing Code is built on, requires every storage water heater to have a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe that ends where you can see it. A valve that drips is doing its job and pointing at a pressure problem.

Two local wear factors: altitude and sediment

Englewood sits at about 5,348 feet. Gas water heaters are designed and listed with altitude in mind, and at this elevation a weak draft or a partly blocked flue shows up quickly as a pilot that goes out or a burner that runs with a yellow, lazy flame. Checking the draft at the vent hood is part of every gas repair we do.

Englewood's tap water holds enough dissolved minerals that the utility is actively working on hardness. Over time those minerals settle as sediment in the bottom of the tank. The rumbling and popping you hear is water trapped under that layer flashing to steam. It wastes gas, stresses the tank bottom and shortens the heater's life.

Flushing a few gallons from the drain valve once a year keeps sediment manageable. On a tank that has never been flushed and is already loud, a flush may not clear it, and it is fair to know that before paying for one.

When repair is the wrong call

We would rather tell you not to fix a heater than sell you a repair that fails next month. A tank leaking from its body cannot be repaired. A gas control valve on a 12-year-old tank can cost a large share of what a new heater would, with no guarantee the tank lasts another year.

If you have a tankless unit rather than a tank, the parts and procedures are different, and the better starting point is our tankless water heater service page.

What affects the cost of water heater repair

We diagnose first and price the repair before starting. These factors move the number.

The part that failed

Thermocouples and anodes are inexpensive. Gas control valves cost considerably more.

Gas or electric

Electric element and thermostat repairs are usually quicker than gas valve replacements.

Tank age

On an older tank we will show you the repair cost next to a replacement quote so you can compare.

Code items

A missing expansion tank or relief valve discharge pipe may need to be corrected at the same visit.

After-hours timing

Night and weekend calls carry a higher rate than a scheduled daytime repair.

Insurers treat water heater repairs as maintenance. Damage from a tank that suddenly fails may be claimable, so photograph it before cleanup.
