Leak Detection in Englewood, CO

Leak detection is the process of finding where water is escaping from a plumbing system before anything is opened up. It uses the water meter, pressure tests, acoustic listening equipment, thermal imaging and moisture meters. In Englewood, CO the goal is simple: cut one small opening in the right spot instead of several wrong ones.

My water bill jumped and I cannot see any water

Most leak calls start with a number on a bill, not a puddle. These are the signs that a hidden leak is the likely cause.

  • A water bill well above the same month last year with no change in household use.
  • The meter dial or leak indicator moving while every fixture in the house is off.
  • A warm patch on a floor or a wall that should be cool.
  • The sound of running water in a wall when nothing is on.
  • A musty smell, soft drywall or paint bubbling low on a wall.
  • A patch of lawn between the street and the house that stays green or soggy in dry weather.

A running toilet is the most common leak inside a house and the cheapest to fix, so it is worth checking first. Add a little food coloring to the tank, then leave it unflushed for 15 minutes. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

What to do while you wait if water is showing

Most leaks are hidden and slow, but some announce themselves. If water is visible, these steps limit the damage safely.

  • If water is dripping through a ceiling near a light fixture or outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker panel.
  • Keep people and pets out from under a ceiling that is sagging or bulging with water.
  • Close the main shutoff if you cannot stop the water at a nearby fixture valve.
  • Put buckets and towels down, and move furniture and electronics out of the way.
  • Take photos of the damage and the meter reading before cleanup, for insurance.

Why a winter leak costs Englewood households twice

In South Englewood Sanitation District No. 1, the sewer portion of the bill is set from winter water use. The district uses the lowest consecutive two-month period between October 1 and May 15, on the reasoning that almost no one is irrigating during those months.

That means a hidden leak running through the winter does not only raise the water bill for those months. It can also raise the base used for the sewer charge. Finding a slow leak in November, rather than in April, protects both numbers.

Englewood Utilities is also replacing water meters across its service area. A new meter is more accurate than a worn one, so some households see a higher bill after a swap that reflects real use, including a leak the old meter under-read. A meter test with every fixture off tells you which situation you are in.

How we find a hidden leak, step by step

  1. Confirm there is a leak. With every fixture off, we watch the meter and the leak indicator. If it moves, water is leaving the system.
  2. Split the system. Closing the main shutoff separates the yard from the house. If the meter still moves, the leak is on the service line outside.
  3. Isolate branches. Inside, we close individual valves and pressure test sections to narrow the leak to hot or cold, and to one area of the house. It is the same principle the IPC (International Plumbing Code) uses for new piping, which must be pressure tested and proven tight before it is covered.
  4. Listen and look. Acoustic microphones pick up the hiss of pressurized water through floors and walls. Thermal imaging shows hot-water leaks as warm patches.
  5. Confirm before cutting. Moisture readings confirm the spot. Only then do we recommend opening a wall or floor, and we tell you exactly where and how large.

Plumbing leak detection finds leaks in pressurized supply lines. Slow drain leaks, such as a cracked drain under a shower, are found with dye tests and cameras instead, and we will tell you which approach fits your symptoms.

What affects the cost of leak detection in Englewood

Detection is quoted separately from the repair, so you know what finding the leak costs before anything is opened.

How many zones to test

A leak confirmed to one bathroom takes far less time than one that could be anywhere in the house or yard.

Indoor versus outdoor

Service line leaks under lawns, driveways or sidewalks can take longer to pinpoint than leaks inside walls.

Access for the repair

Once found, the repair cost depends on what covers the pipe: drywall, tile, concrete or soil.

Pipe material

Galvanized pipe may need more replacement than the leak itself, because neighboring joints are often weak too.

Timing

An active leak with visible water is handled 24/7. A slow, stable leak is cheaper to schedule on a weekday.

Many homeowners policies pay for access to find a covered leak and for the resulting sudden water damage, but not for the leaking pipe itself. Gradual leaks are often excluded. Keep your leak detection report, since insurers usually ask for it.
