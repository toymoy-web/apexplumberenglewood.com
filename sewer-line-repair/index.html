Sewer Line Repair in Englewood, CO

Sewer line repair fixes a damaged section of the private sewer lateral, the pipe that carries waste from your house to the sanitation district's main. It covers spot repairs, joint repairs and cured-in-place lining. In Englewood, CO the owner is responsible for that pipe all the way to the tap on the district main, so the repair decision is yours to make with good information.

Your lateral, your responsibility

Englewood is served by more than one sanitation district. South Platte Renew keeps an online map that shows which district covers a given address. South Englewood Sanitation District No. 1 states its rule plainly: the sewer line from the building up to and including the tap into the district main belongs to the owner.

That district also says that when a backup is caused by a problem in its own main, it sends a cleanup contractor. It does not pay for personal loss, reconstruction or flooring. If the problem is in your lateral, cleanup and repair are yours.

The practical takeaway is to find out what condition your lateral is in before it fails. A camera inspection costs a fraction of an emergency excavation, and it tells you whether the pipe needs anything at all.

Signs your sewer line needs more than cleaning

Cleaning clears a blockage. These signs point to a damaged pipe that will keep causing blockages until it is repaired.

  • Backups that return within months of the line being cleared.
  • Gurgling toilets or drains when a washer or tub empties.
  • Sewage odor in the yard or basement with no visible backup.
  • A sunken strip or soggy patch in the lawn along the line's path.
  • Camera footage showing a crack, an offset joint, a sag holding water or roots entering at one spot.

If a camera shows the pipe is sound and the problem is grease or roots in otherwise intact pipe, hydro jetting may be all you need.

Repair methods compared

The camera footage decides which of these fits. Many laterals need only one defect addressed.

MethodWhat it fixesTrade-offs
Spot repair by excavationA single crack, break or root-entry joint.One pit instead of a trench, but depth adds cost.

When the camera shows damage along most of the line, repairing sections one at a time rarely pays off, and sewer line replacement becomes the better value.

How a sewer repair is diagnosed and done

  1. Clear the line. A blocked line gets cleared first, which lets the camera reach the district main.
  2. Camera and locate. A camera runs from the cleanout to the district main, and a locator marks each defect's position and depth on the surface.
  3. Choose the method. You see the footage and the options, priced, before any work is scheduled.
  4. Permit and locates. Excavation work is permitted as required, and Colorado 811 marks buried utilities before digging.
  5. Repair and verify. After the repair, the camera goes back down to confirm the result, and you get the final footage.

What affects the cost of sewer line repair

Sewer repairs are quoted from the camera footage, not from a guess.

Depth of the pipe

Deeper laterals mean bigger pits and more shoring, which adds cost quickly.

What sits above

Lawn is easy to restore. Driveways, sidewalks, patios and mature trees add restoration work.

Method

Lining avoids digging but has its own setup costs. A single shallow spot repair can be cheaper.

Pipe material

Clay, cast iron and plastic behave differently when repaired or lined.

Emergency timing

An active sewage backup is cleared 24/7. The repair itself is best scheduled after the camera look.

Many homeowners policies exclude damage from sewer backups unless a sewer backup rider was added, and the lateral repair itself is generally treated as maintenance. Check your coverage before a backup happens.
