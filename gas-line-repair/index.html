Gas Line Installation & Repair in Englewood, CO

Gas line installation and repair covers the natural gas piping on your side of the meter. That includes new lines for ranges, dryers, fireplaces, grills and tankless water heaters, as well as repairs to leaking or damaged piping. In Englewood, CO this work is permitted and inspected, and the utility is always the first call when you smell gas.

What to do if you smell gas

Natural gas has an added odor that smells like rotten eggs so leaks can be noticed. Treat that smell as an emergency.

  • Get everyone out of the building, including pets.
  • Do not use light switches, phones, doorbells or anything that could spark while inside.
  • Do not try to find the leak yourself.
  • From a safe distance, call Xcel Energy's gas emergency line or 911.
  • After the utility has shut off gas or made it safe, call a licensed plumber for the repair.

Who handles what: Xcel Energy and your plumber

Xcel Energy delivers natural gas to Englewood homes and owns the service line and meter. Everything after the meter, the piping through your house to each appliance, belongs to the property owner and is plumbing work.

That split matters in an emergency. The utility responds to a suspected leak and makes the area safe, and it may shut off gas at the meter. A plumber then repairs the owner-side piping, tests it, and arranges any inspection needed before gas is restored.

New gas lines for appliances and upgrades

Most new gas line requests come from an appliance upgrade. Typical examples are a gas range where there was electric, a gas dryer, an outdoor grill or fire pit, or a tankless water heater that needs more gas than the old tank did.

Sizing matters. A line must deliver enough gas for every appliance on it running at once, and long runs need larger pipe. Tankless water heaters are the most common reason an existing line turns out to be too small. Our water heater installation page explains how that affects a tank-to-tankless switch.

In Colorado, fuel gas piping follows the state's Fuel Gas Code, built on the 2021 International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC). Among other things, that code requires corrugated stainless steel tubing (CSST) to be bonded to the home's electrical grounding system, which is itself installed under the National Electrical Code (NEC). We check bonding on any CSST we work on.

What affects the cost of gas line work

Gas line jobs are quoted after we see the route. These factors move the price.

Length and route

A short basement run is simpler than one through finished walls or to the back yard.

Pipe size

Larger appliances or long runs need bigger pipe, which costs more.

Permit and inspection

Gas work is permitted, which adds a fee and an inspection visit.

Pressure testing

New and repaired piping is pressure tested before gas is turned back on.

Emergency repairs

Repairs after a utility shutoff can be scheduled quickly, including after hours.

Gas line installation is not covered by insurance. Damage caused by a sudden gas-related event may be, so talk to your insurer if that happens.
