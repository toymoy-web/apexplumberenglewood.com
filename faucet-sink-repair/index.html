Signs your faucet or sink needs attention

Most faucet and sink problems start small. These are the ones worth fixing before they cause damage.

  • A spout that keeps dripping even with the handle shut tight.
  • Water pooling around the base of the faucet or seeping under it.
  • A handle that is stiff, loose or hard to set to the right temperature.
  • Low flow from one faucet while others in the house are normal.
  • Moisture, stains or a musty smell inside the cabinet under the sink.

Scale, cartridges and Englewood water

Englewood treats its own water from City Ditch and the South Platte River, and the city is working to reduce its hardness. Minerals in the water build up in aerators, cartridges and valve seats. That is the most common cause of low flow at a single faucet and of handles that stiffen over the years.

Low flow at one faucet is often fixed by cleaning or replacing the aerator and cartridge. Low flow at every fixture in the house is a different problem, usually corroded supply pipe or a failing pressure reducing valve, which our repiping page discusses.

Modern cartridge faucets can often be rebuilt with the manufacturer's part. Older compression faucets with rubber washers can be repaired too, but when the valve seat is badly worn, replacing the faucet is usually the better value.

Since 2016, new bathroom faucets sold in Colorado must carry the EPA WaterSense label. They use less water while still delivering a comfortable flow.

The IPC (International Plumbing Code) also calls for a shutoff valve on the supply to each sink, so a faucet can be worked on without shutting off the whole house. Many older Englewood kitchens and baths are missing one or have valves that no longer close.

What to do if a sink or faucet is leaking now

These steps stop most sink leaks until a repair can be made.

  • Close both shutoff valves under the sink by turning them clockwise.
  • If the valves will not close or keep dripping, close the main water shutoff.
  • Empty the cabinet and put a bucket and towels under the leak.
  • If water has reached an outlet or a disposal cord, switch off that circuit at the breaker before touching anything.
  • Leave the cabinet doors open so the base can dry, and photograph any damage.

Leaks under the sink

Water inside the sink cabinet comes from one of three places: the supply lines and shutoff valves, the faucet itself, or the drain. Running the faucet with the drain closed, then opening it, usually shows which one. Slip-joint drain fittings loosen over time and are a quick fix, while a corroded metal trap in an older home is better replaced with a new one.

Kitchen sinks with a disposal have more connections, and a leak at the disposal's flange or body is covered on our garbage disposal repair page.
