Sewer Line Replacement in Englewood, CO

Sewer line replacement is installing a new sewer lateral from the house to the sanitation district's main, replacing the old pipe along its whole length. It is the right call when the old line has collapsed, sagged badly or is damaged in many places. In Englewood, CO it is usually done by open trench or by trenchless pipe bursting, depending on what sits above the line.

Replacement is a big decision, and it should be driven by what the camera shows. This is how we frame it.

Why so many Englewood laterals are reaching the end

Englewood's housing grew fastest after World War II, and city-data puts the median house build year at 1955. Sewer laterals from that period were commonly vitrified clay or cast iron. Clay comes in short sections joined every few feet, and each joint is a place for roots to enter or for the pipe to shift.

Some homes built nationally from the late 1940s into the early 1970s also used bituminous fiber pipe, often called Orangeburg. It was cheap and easy to install, but it softens and loses its shape as it ages. When a camera shows a flattened, blistered pipe wall, that is usually the cause, and it cannot be lined.

Many older Englewood streets are lined with large, mature trees. Their roots follow moisture to the joints of old laterals, which is why the same houses tend to call about backups year after year. Until a replacement is scheduled, hydro jetting can keep a root-prone line flowing.

Open trench or trenchless pipe bursting

Both methods produce a new, continuous line. The difference is what happens to your yard.

FactorOpen trenchPipe bursting
DiggingA trench along the full route.Two pits, one at each end.
Driveways and treesConcrete is cut and replaced, roots may be cut.Usually passes under both.
Fixing grade problemsCan correct a sagging line.Follows the old path, including its grade.
CostOften lower for short, shallow, open runs.Often lower where surface restoration would be costly.

Pipe bursting pulls a new plastic pipe through the old one while splitting the old pipe outward. It does not fix a line that was laid at the wrong slope, so the camera footage decides if it is suitable.

A typical replacement from start to finish

  1. Review the footage. You see the defects and their locations and approve the method.
  2. Permits and locates. The work is permitted, the district's requirements for the tap are confirmed, and Colorado 811 marks buried utilities.
  3. Excavate. Pits or a trench are opened, and the connection points at the house and the main are exposed.
  4. Install. New pipe goes in with a cleanout near the house for future maintenance.
  5. Inspect and camera. The line is inspected, a final camera run confirms it, and the ground is restored.

Most residential replacements take one to three days, and the house can usually use its drains again each evening once the new line is connected.

What sets the price of a sewer replacement

Replacements are quoted from footage and a site visit. These factors move the number the most.

Length and depth

Longer and deeper laterals need more pipe, more excavation and sometimes shoring.

Method

Pipe bursting and open trench each win on price in different situations.

Surface restoration

Driveways, walks, retaining walls and landscaping above the line add cost.

Connection at the main

Work at the tap or in the street involves additional permits and requirements.

Cleanout installation

Adding an accessible cleanout is a small cost that saves money on every future service call.

Sewer line replacement is generally treated as maintenance and is not covered by standard homeowners policies. Separate service line coverage is sold by some insurers and utilities.
